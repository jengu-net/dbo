## One archive, both ways

Backup is not a feature beside export. It is the same operation: a consistent
snapshot of everything the tenant is — its records, its history, its
definitions, its credentials and its work — in one file. The thing that runs
nightly is the thing that leaves, so an escape route is never tested for the
first time on the worst day of a relationship.

## The key is the tenant's

The archive is sealed under the tenant owner's key, which the store does not
hold: asked for one without the key, it refuses and says so. An operator who
takes a backup is a custodian, not a reader, and so is whoever stores the file
afterwards. Identifying data inside it is still under each person's own key, so
an erasure reaches archives already written.

## Coming back is a ceremony

An archive enters a store by one path only: its contents are checked against
the root it was signed over, and both signatures verify, or nothing is written.
A refusal names what was wrong. Restored somewhere else, the tenant keeps its
identities — the ids its records were written under and the clients that may
reach it.
