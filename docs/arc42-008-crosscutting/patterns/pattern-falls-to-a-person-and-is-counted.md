**Intent — turn the work that automation did not take into a measurement
instead of an embarrassment.**

## You are

Automating a process that is partly automatable. Some steps have a runner.
Some will have one next year. Some should stay a human decision forever, and
pretending otherwise would be the actual mistake.

You would also like to know which is which, ranked by how much time each one
costs. That question is usually answered by hiring somebody to go and watch.

## The question

Where does work go when nothing automated will take it, and what do you learn
from the fact that it went there?

## The forces

- Treating human fall-through as an error produces alarms for the normal case.
- Treating it as invisible means the backlog of unautomated work is never
  quantified, and automation gets prioritised by whoever argues best.
- Some steps must reach a person, so the mechanism cannot be a stopgap.

## Therefore

**Make the fall-through an ordinary outcome, and count it — per step, and per
part of the organisation.**

--8<-- "assets/diagrams/pattern-falls-to-a-person-and-is-counted.svg"

<p class="diagram-caption">The count is the deliverable, not the complaint.</p>

What you get is a list nobody else can give you: exactly which stages of which
processes are still costing somebody an afternoon, how often, and in which
part of the organisation. That is an automation roadmap derived from what
actually happened, rather than from a workshop.

It also changes what "automated" means operationally. A step is not automated
or manual as a property of its design; it is automated to the extent that a
runner took it this month, which is a number that moves as runners are
deployed and as volumes change.

## What each reader gets

- **A regulator** sees which decisions are still being made by a person, which
  is often the answer they wanted.
- **A security officer** sees where human access to records is actually being
  exercised, and can concentrate review there.
- **An administrator** staffs from a measured queue rather than from
  complaints.
- **The business** ranks automation investment by the hours each step is
  costing now.
